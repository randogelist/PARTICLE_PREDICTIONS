1. INPUT REGISTER AND MEANING OF "LATTICE"
======================================================================

There are three distinct mathematical objects.

(A) An address set labels an orthonormal basis.
(B) An integral compatibility lattice describes integer multiplicative
    deformations of positive energy coordinates.
(C) A resonance object would require a dynamical definition of poles,
    widths, and the associated state/channel interpretation.

The energy set {n,1/n : n >= 1} is not an additive lattice in R.
The word "lattice" in the exact lattice theorems refers to object (B).

GENERAL COMPATIBILITY INPUTS:

C1. An integer r >= 1 and an ordered partition into block sizes
        d_1,...,d_r >= 1,        N = sum_j d_j.
    These sizes are abstract coordinate blocks, not derived particle counts.

C2. Fixed dimensionless positive centers c_1,...,c_r > 0.
    Their common rescaling is redundant with the energy normalization.
    One may fix c_r = 1 without loss of generality, leaving r-1 ratios.

C3. The common-center product law for positive coordinates E_{j,i}:
        product_{i=1}^{d_j} E_{j,i} = (E_* c_j)^{d_j},
        j=1,...,r,             E_* > 0.
    Geometric-mean preservation is a model premise.

C4. Integer exponents are taken in these specified multiplicative energy
    coordinates. This fixes the integral structure used below.
    It does not discretize the actual energy manifold.

C5. For Gram matrices, lengths, and determinants, use the ordinary
    Euclidean metric sum_{j,i} dx_{j,i}^2 in logarithmic coordinates.
    Another metric changes the metric lattice invariants.

Units:
E_{j,i} and E_* have energy units; c_j and all exponential factors are
dimensionless. Every logarithm below has a dimensionless argument.

2. COMPLETE POSITIVE MANIFOLD AND PARAMETER COUNT
======================================================================

Definition:
M(d,c) is the set of E in (0,infinity)^N for which there exists E_* > 0
satisfying C3.

THEOREM PL-01 (complete parameterization).
Every E in M(d,c) has the unique representation

    E_{j,i} = E_* c_j exp(z_{j,i}),
    sum_{i=1}^{d_j} z_{j,i} = 0 for every j.                 (2.1)

Here the c_j are fixed, including their overall normalization.

Proof.
If (2.1) holds, taking the product in block j gives C3.
Conversely C3 gives

    E_* = c_j^{-1}(product_i E_{j,i})^{1/d_j},               (2.2)

independently of j. Define z_{j,i}=log(E_{j,i}/(E_*c_j)).
The logarithm of C3 gives sum_i z_{j,i}=0.
Formula (2.2) fixes E_* uniquely, and then fixes every z. QED.

Choose an arbitrary reference energy E_ref > 0 only to write coordinates:

    x_{j,i} = log(E_{j,i}/(E_ref c_j)),
    t = log(E_*/E_ref).

Then

    x = t 1_N + z,
    z in Z_R := direct_sum_j {u in R^{d_j}: sum_i u_i=0}.    (2.3)

The subspaces R 1_N and Z_R are orthogonal in C5, so

    V_d := R 1_N orthogonal_sum Z_R,
    dim V_d = 1 + sum_j(d_j-1) = N-r+1.                    (2.4)

For fixed E_*, the dimension is N-r.

Changing E_ref translates t; it changes none of the compatibility laws,
tangent vectors, lattice invariants, or physical energy ratios.

THEOREM PL-02 (center elimination and independent constraints).
For j<r, C3 with E_* eliminated is equivalent on positive energies to

    (product_i E_{j,i})^{d_r} / (product_i E_{r,i})^{d_j}
             = (c_j/c_r)^{d_j d_r}.                        (2.5)

These are exactly r-1 independent logarithmic equations.

Proof.
Taking logs of (2.5) gives equality of normalized block means.
Positive roots therefore reconstruct the common E_* by (2.2).
The jth equation uses block j and reference block r; an arbitrary linear
combination vanishing on all nonreference blocks has all coefficients
zero. Thus the equations have rank r-1. QED.

INPUT MINIMALITY WITHIN THIS FAMILY:
With centers fixed, one scale plus N-r splitting coordinates is complete.
Every splitting coordinate can vary freely while preserving all others.
Thus no splitting value follows from C3 alone.

If the r-1 independent center ratios are also free, the total number of
continuous parameters becomes

    1 + (N-r) + (r-1) = N.                                 (2.6)

Indeed, for any positive E, let its block geometric means be G_j.
Taking E_*=G_r and c_j=G_j/G_r, with c_r=1, reconstructs (2.1).
Therefore allowing all centers to vary admits ALL positive spectra.

Consequently:
Free centers give a universal representation but no restrictive mass-ratio
prediction. Fixed or derived center ratios are necessary for this family
to impose nontrivial spectral constraints.

This is a relative parameter-count theorem for C1-C3.
It is not a claim of globally minimal axioms for all possible physics.

3. INTEGRAL EXPONENT LATTICE
======================================================================

For u>0 and v in Z^N, consider the multiplicative deformation

    E_{j,i} -> E_{j,i} u^{v_{j,i}}.

It stays in M(d,c) for every u>0 precisely when the block averages of v
are all equal. The common E_* is then multiplied by u^t, where

    t = (1/d_j) sum_i v_{j,i}, independently of j.

Definition:

    L_d := V_d intersect Z^N
         = {v in Z^N: all block averages are equal}.         (3.1)

This is the integral compatibility lattice. It describes allowed integer
exponents, not a restriction z in L_d on the real coordinates in (2.1).

For j<r define integer equations

    (Qv)_j = d_r sum_i v_{j,i} - d_j sum_i v_{r,i}.           (3.2)

Then

    L_d = ker(Q: Z^N -> Z^{r-1}).                           (3.3)

For r=1, Q has no rows and L_d=Z^N.

THEOREM PL-03 (rank and primitivity).
L_d has rank N-r+1 and is primitive/saturated in Z^N.

Proof.
Q has rank r-1 over Q by the same block-independence argument as PL-02,
so its rational kernel has dimension N-r+1.
For saturation, if k v belongs to ker Q for v in Z^N and nonzero integer k,
then k Qv=0 in the torsion-free group Z^{r-1}; hence Qv=0. QED.

The partition and multiplicative coordinates are part of the input.
The product laws alone do not privilege every possible choice of integral
coordinate system or logarithmic metric.

======================================================================
4. SATURATION INDEX AND GENERAL DETERMINANT
======================================================================

Let

    g := gcd(d_1,...,d_r),

    A_{d_j-1} := {v in Z^{d_j}: sum_i v_i=0},
    A_0 := {0},

    L_0 := Z 1_N orthogonal_sum (direct_sum_j A_{d_j-1}).    (4.1)

L_0 is always a sublattice of L_d of full rank.

THEOREM PL-04 (exact saturation).
The common-average map t:L_d -> Q has image (1/g)Z and kernel
direct_sum_j A_{d_j-1}. Consequently

    L_d/L_0 isomorphic to Z/gZ,
    [L_d:L_0] = g.                                         (4.2)

Proof.
If t is a common block average, then d_j t is an integer for every j.
Choose integers b_j with sum_j b_j d_j=g (Bezout).
Then g t=sum_j b_j(d_j t) is an integer, so t belongs to (1/g)Z.

Conversely, for t=k/g choose in each block the integer vector whose first
coordinate is k d_j/g and whose other coordinates vanish.
Its block average is k/g, proving surjectivity.
The kernel t=0 is exactly the direct sum of zero-sum root lattices.
The image of L_0 is Z, and L_0 is the preimage of Z, proving (4.2). QED.

THEOREM PL-05 (Gram determinant).

    det(L_d) = N (product_j d_j) / g^2.                     (4.3)

Here det is the determinant of a Gram matrix in the metric C5, not its
square root.

Proof.
The vector 1_N has squared norm N and is orthogonal to all zero-sum blocks.
In A_{d-1}, the simple-root basis e_i-e_{i+1} has tridiagonal Gram matrix
with diagonal 2 and neighboring entries -1.
If D_k denotes its k by k determinant, expansion gives
D_k=2D_{k-1}-D_{k-2}, D_0=1, D_1=2; therefore D_k=k+1.
Thus det A_{d-1}=d, including det A_0=1.

It follows that det L_0=N product_j d_j.
A sublattice of index g has covolume g times that of its superlattice,
so its Gram determinant is multiplied by g^2.
Apply PL-04 to obtain (4.3). QED.

COROLLARY PL-06 (coprime block sizes).
If g=1, then

    L_d = <N> orthogonal_sum A_{d_1-1} ... A_{d_r-1}.         (4.4)

The discriminant group is

    L_d^*/L_d isomorphic to
    Z/NZ direct_sum (direct_sum_j Z/d_j Z).                 (4.5)

Proof.
Equation (4.2) gives equality of L_d and L_0.
For <N>, the dual generator is 1_N/N and the quotient is Z/NZ.
For A_{d-1}, the vector ((d-1)/d,-1/d,...,-1/d) is dual to the
integer zero-sum lattice, and its class has exact order d.
The quotient has order det A_{d-1}=d, so this class generates it.
Orthogonal sums give direct sums of dual quotients. QED.

Caution:
For g>1, (4.4) and (4.5) need not hold: L_d is a genuine overlattice.
The general determinant formula (4.3) still holds.
If E_* is kept as an additional multiplicative coordinate whose exponent
must also be integral, the projected energy exponents give L_0.
Eliminating that extra integral-exponent requirement gives the saturated L_d.

COROLLARY PL-06b (center non-identifiability from the tangent lattice).
For fixed d and the coordinate/metric conventions C4-C5, L_d and every
invariant computed from it are independent of all positive centers c_j.
In particular, when r>=2, varying a relative center while keeping the
others fixed changes unsplit energy ratios without changing L_d.

Proof.
The defining equations (3.1)-(3.2) contain only d and v, not c.
At z=0, (2.1) gives E_{j,i}/E_{k,l}=c_j/c_k, which can vary freely.
Thus the same lattice supports different center ratios. QED.

Consequently a center-selection law cannot be extracted from the
unmarked metric lattice alone. Additional structure is required.

7. POSITIVE SELF-ADJOINT OPERATOR AND FULL SPECTRUM
======================================================================

Define

    H_0 e_{n,p}=E_* n^p e_{n,p},

    D(H_0)={u in H: sum_{n,p} E_*^2 n^{2p}|u_{n,p}|^2
                                    is finite}.            (7.1)

THEOREM PL-07.
H_0 is densely defined, positive, and self-adjoint.
Finite-support vectors form a core.

Proof.
Finite-support vectors lie in the domain and are dense.
All diagonal coefficients are real and positive, so symmetry and
nonnegative quadratic form follow by convergent Hilbert-space pairing.

Testing a vector in the adjoint domain against each basis vector forces
its adjoint image to have coordinates E_* n^p u_{n,p}.
That image belongs to H exactly when the condition in (7.1) holds.
Thus the adjoint domain is D(H_0), and H_0^*=H_0.

Finite-coordinate truncations converge both in H and after applying H_0
by convergence of the two defining squared sums. QED.

THEOREM PL-08 (complete spectrum).

    sigma(H_0)
      = E_* ({0} union N_{>0} union {1/q:q>=1}).             (7.2)

Zero is not an eigenvalue. Every positive displayed spectral value is
an eigenvalue.

Proof.
A diagonal real operator has spectrum equal to the closure of its
diagonal values: off that closure, division by E_*n^p-z is uniformly
bounded and gives an everywhere-defined inverse into D(H_0).
On a diagonal value a basis eigenvector prevents invertibility.
At a finite accumulation value, a sequence of basis vectors has
||(H_0-z)e_{n,p}|| tending to zero, preventing a bounded inverse.

Here the inverse-square values n^{-2} are already reciprocal values
q^{-1} with q=n^2. The integers have no finite accumulation point; the
reciprocals accumulate only at zero. All coefficients are positive,
so the kernel is zero. QED.

THEOREM PL-09 (bare multiplicities).
For x=E/E_*, at positive spectral values,

    mult(1)=3;
    mult(m)=1 for integers m>=2;
    mult(1/q)=1+1_{q is a perfect square}, q>=2.             (7.3)

Proof.
Only n=1 lies on all three sheets.
For values above 1 only the positive sheet contributes.
For q>=2 the inverse sheet gives one state, and the inverse-square
sheet gives one additional state precisely when q=n^2. QED.

These are model multiplicities; physical spin, polarization, color,
and other representation multiplicities have not been supplied.

8. COMPLETE CUTOFF DESCRIPTION AND FINITE WINDOW COUNT
======================================================================

Let Lambda>0, rho=Lambda/E_*.

THEOREM PL-10 (upper-cutoff enumeration).
The complete label set with E_{n,p}<=Lambda is

    p=+1: 1 <= n <= floor(rho);
    p=-1: n >= max(1,ceil(rho^{-1}));
    p=-2: n >= max(1,ceil(rho^{-1/2})).                      (8.1)

Proof.
Divide the energy inequality by E_*>0 and solve n^p<=rho
on each sheet. For p<0, inversion reverses the inequality. QED.

For every Lambda>0 this is countably infinite, and the spectral projection
onto (0,Lambda] has infinite rank.
Nevertheless, (8.1) is a complete finite symbolic description of every
label in that infinite set. A finite-list requirement is stronger.

Now take 0<epsilon<=Lambda, and set

    a=epsilon/E_*,       b=Lambda/E_*,

    C(u,v)=max(0, floor(v)-max(1,ceil(u))+1).                (8.2)

THEOREM PL-11 (exact finite-window counts).

    N_states([epsilon,Lambda])
       = C(a,b) + C(b^{-1},a^{-1})
                  + C(b^{-1/2},a^{-1/2}),                  (8.3)

    N_distinct([epsilon,Lambda])
       = C(a,b) + C(b^{-1},a^{-1})
                  - 1_{a<=1<=b}.                          (8.4)

Proof.
The three integer intervals are respectively
[a,b], [b^{-1},a^{-1}], and [b^{-1/2},a^{-1/2}].
C counts their positive integers, so (8.3) counts basis labels.
All inverse-square energies already occur on the inverse sheet.
The only overlap between positive-integer and reciprocal energies is 1,
present exactly when a<=1<=b. This proves (8.4). QED.

23. EXACT INTERACTION AND DECAY CONSERVATION CRITERION
======================================================================

To turn a label into an additive charge, specify an abelian group G and
a map q:S->G on the species/state labels, including antiparticle rules.
For an additive internal charge use q(antiparticle)=-q(particle).

A reaction with incoming labels a_1,...,a_k and outgoing labels
b_1,...,b_m preserves q precisely when

    sum_r q(a_r) = sum_s q(b_s)               in G.

For real charges, on finite-particle vectors let Q=sum_a q(a)N_a.
A creation-annihilation vertex V with those input/output labels obeys

    [Q,V] = (sum_out q - sum_in q) V.

neutral vertices commute with Q on their common algebraic core. An
operator-level conservation theorem also needs the appropriate domains
and a compatible self-adjoint realization; no full interacting operator
is constructed here.

For finite G, use its characters chi:G->U(1). On a multi-particle state,

    U_chi V U_chi^(-1) = chi(sum_out q - sum_in q) V.

Because finite-group characters separate points, neutrality in G is
equivalent to invariance under all U_chi. The correct exact symmetry
criterion is [H,U_chi]=0 for every character, not commutation with an
arbitrarily chosen integer representative of a modular charge.

Equivalently, if the actual reactions supply integer columns

    real additive conserved charges = ker(B^T),

48. EXACT RECONSTRUCTION AND THE TWO GENERATED ALGEBRAS
======================================================================

Each row is one Jordan block with its own lambda_j. Its minimal
polynomial is (x-lambda_j)^d_j. These factors are relatively prime
for different rows, so the minimal polynomial of B is their product.
It has degree sixteen and equals the characteristic polynomial.
The Chinese remainder theorem gives

    C[B] = C[x]/(product_j (x-lambda_j)^d_j)
         isomorphic to (C[t]/(t^2))^5 + C[t]/(t^3) + C^3.

This algebra of polynomials in B is commutative of dimension sixteen.
For a concrete reconstruction choose a polynomial f_j congruent to 1
modulo (x-lambda_j)^d_j and 0 modulo every other block factor.
Then R_j=f_j(B) is precisely the orthogonal block projection, since
each congruence evaluates to its constant on the corresponding block.
In particular D=sum_j lambda_j R_j and S=B-D are recovered exactly.
This is the elementary primary decomposition argument [F16-1].

Let Alg*(B) denote the unital algebra generated by B and its adjoint.
In the chosen orthonormal basis I-SS^dagger projects onto the first
state of every row. For r,s=1,...,d_j,

    F_{j,11}=R_j(I-SS^dagger),
    F_{j,rs}=S^{r-1} F_{j,11} (S^dagger)^{s-1}
            = |j,r><j,s|.

This proves that all sixteen individual state projectors and every
within-block transition are present. Conversely, both B and B^dagger
are block diagonal. Hence

    Alg*(B) = M_2(C)^5 + M_3(C) + C^3,   dim_C=32.          (48.1)

Its center is the nine-dimensional span of R_j. The sixteen
F_{j,rr} span the full algebra of diagonal address observables.
Using the table on these projectors reconstructs P, N and the
original base-four words. This reconstruction retains the chosen
sheet/address correspondence as an input. The matrix spectrum alone
does not name species or fix a labeling of its basis.

The associated mass Lie algebra is recovered inside (48.1) by
requiring equal normalized block traces. Its dimension is twenty-four:

    g_16=C I_16 + sl(2,C)^5 + sl(3,C).

Real integral diagonals satisfying that trace condition recover
L_d=<16> orthogonal_sum A_1^5 orthogonal_sum A_2. With the marked
energies lambda_j, all admitted positive block mass matrices can be
written K D exp(X) for Hermitian X with traceless block restrictions
and no off-block entries. The bare mass occurs at X=0.

52. THE SHEET GRADES THE MATRIX ALGEBRA

In the basis of Section 46 let P=diag(p_a). For a matrix unit
E_ab=|a><b| in the same mass fiber,

    [H_0,E_ab]=0,        [P,E_ab]=(p_a-p_b) E_ab.

Giving E_ab the integer degree p_a-p_b defines a grading. Nonzero

    [P,H_0]=0,        [P,S]=S+E_(13,12) != 0.

53. THREE CHARGED-CURRENT VERTICES FORCE SHEET-ONLY CHARGES TO BE CONSTANT
======================================================================

Suppose q=f(p) on the marked particle seeds, with values in any abelian
group. Let the conventional W^+ have a single grade w, antiparticles
opposite grade, and all interaction coefficients grade zero. A nonzero
CKM entry V_ij for W^+ coupling an up-type quark i to a down-type
quark j requires w=f(p_i)-f(p_j) [IR1].

    entry       sheets (up,down)          required w
    V_ud             (-2,-2)              0
    V_cd             (-1,-2)              f(-1)-f(-2)
    V_cb             (-1,+1)              f(-1)-f(+1)

All three entries occur in the ordinary charged current. Equating
their required w yields w=0 and f(-2)=f(-1)=f(+1). Thus a conserved
function of sheet alone cannot distinguish sheets, even for finite
abelian values or after a nonlinear relabeling. This proof needs
neither numerical CKM values nor a partner transition. For raw p,
the first two entries already require incompatible values 0 and 1.

For comparison, allowing an independently assigned species charge
changes the answer. With connected quark and lepton charged-current
support as in Section 30, Dirac-type bookkeeping, and neutral
within-fiber partner links, assign x_U to up-type quarks, x_D to
down-type quarks, x_L to charged leptons and each seed's grade to its
partners. Necessity by cancellation along each support graph and
sufficiency by substitution give

    q(W^+)=x_U-x_D,  q(W^-)=-q(W^+),  q(nu)=x_L+x_U-x_D.

These three parameters in the chosen abelian group describe precisely
the assignments on this species list for these vertices. Further mass
terms or interactions may reduce the freedom. In units of positive
elementary charge, x_U=2/3, x_D=-1/3, x_L=-1 yield q(W^+)=1 and
q(nu)=0. Electric charge therefore fits this algebra and these vertices
without needing a new sheet charge.

54. THE SEPARATE COMMON MATTER GRADE UNDER ELECTROWEAK TOPOLOGY
======================================================================

compatible with the ordinary connected weak currents. Its common
generator g_0 in Z/16 assigns +1 to every matter particle, -1 to its

already equals F=3 B_bar + L_lep modulo 16. Here B_bar is baryon
number (1/3 per quark) and L_lep is total lepton number (1 per lepton).

assignment; whether a process preserves it is a separate calculation.

Add as physical input the standard three-generation electroweak
topological selection rule with unchanged chiral field content. A
unit change of winding has Delta B_bar=Delta L_lep=3 up to an overall

    (Z/16)/<12> = Z/4.

    F = 3(B_bar-L_lep)+4L_lep,

anomaly coefficient would have to be computed for that theory. No
collider observation or all-interactions conservation claim enters.

              = A_1^5 orthogonal_sum A_2.

    ker(pi_*) is contained in R.                            (63.1)

67. A BASIS-INDEPENDENT STATISTIC FOR THE MASS OPERATOR
======================================================================

Fix the flavor projectors R_f, dimensions d_f=Tr(R_f), and the
observation map before scoring data. On the central bare mass model

    m_f(H) = Tr(R_f H)/d_f = K lambda_f.

Under simultaneous unitary conjugation of H and R_f the trace is
unchanged. This statistic measures a mean over a mass fiber. For
an experimentally resolved individual mass use its matched spectral
projection or eigenvalue. A split fiber requires an explicit choice
of which physical observable is compared.

Given a test set T and anchor e, define r_j=lambda_j/lambda_e.
For y the vector of measurements in common units, let L have one row
per test with (Ly)_j=y_j-r_j y_e. Then L lambda=0. Under the null
E[y]=K lambda and joint covariance C one obtains exactly

    z=Ly,    E[z]=0,    V=Cov(z)=L C L^T.

Proof: apply linearity of expectation and expand the centered outer
product. This includes anchor uncertainty and shared correlations.
For the first two-mass contrast z=y_mu-256 y_e its variance is
C_mumu + 256^2 C_ee - 512 C_mue. A separately published ratio can
instead be compared directly to 256 using its own quoted uncertainty.

If the joint errors are Gaussian with known C, diagonalize V on its
positive-eigenvalue subspace. After whitening, the coordinates are
independent standard normals. Hence

    T=z^T V^+ z,      T ~ chi-square(rank(V)).

Here V^+ is the Moore-Penrose inverse. A residual in an exact null
direction with nonzero component violates the null support and must be
reported separately from T. The contrast has already eliminated K.
For one contrast of positive variance this gives the two-sided normal
test from Z=z/sqrt(V). These reference distributions require the stated
sampling assumptions. Systematics or theory errors require a specified
nuisance model or explicit bounds. Estimated covariances, scan choices,
and nonlinear fits may require a different calibration.

71. SPATIAL ENERGY DENSITY ON THE RISING BARE SHEET
======================================================================

Let E(n,p) = E_* n^p be the bare rest-energy assignment of section 6.
For increasing n, the p=+1 sheet rises without bound. Fix one occupied
state per assigned physical cell and write V(n,p)>0 for its volume.
The mean rest-energy density assigned to that cell is u(n,p)=E(n,p)/V(n,p).
On the rising sheet this gives the exact comparison

    u(m,+1)/u(n,+1) = (m/n) V(n,+1)/V(m,+1).               (71.1)

An energy spectrum alone leaves this ratio undetermined. For example,
a common fixed volume gives linear growth. More generally, any positive
density sequence b_n is compatible with the same rising energies by
setting V(n,+1)=E_* n/b_n.

PL-45 (conditional reduced-Compton-cell estimate).
Assign the length ell(E)=hbar c/E to a state of rest energy E and set
V(E)=kappa ell(E)^3 with one fixed dimensionless shape factor kappa>0.
Taking ell as the side of a cube gives kappa=1. Direct substitution yields

    u(E) = E^4 / [kappa (hbar c)^3],
    u(n,p) = u_* n^(4p),   u_* = E_*^4/[kappa (hbar c)^3].  (71.2)

On the rising sheet, n -> 2n gives 16 times the density. For the radix-4
word operation that appends a zero, n -> 4n gives 256 times the density.
For consecutive levels, the ratio is (1+1/n)^4 and tends to one.
On the inverse sheets the corresponding powers of n are -4 and -8.

With kappa=1 and the electron calibration E_*=K_e from (14.2),

    ell(n,+1) = 0.09428/n fm,
    u(n,+1)   = 2.498e3 n^4 GeV/fm^3
              = 4.002e38 n^4 J/m^3.                        (71.3)

PL-47 (conditional macroscopic descent law).
Set x=Mc^2/E_* for a black hole of mass M. The continuous x agrees

Hawking's temperature and the Schwarzschild area give
    k_B T_H = E_P^2/(8 pi E_* x),         E_P^2 = hbar c^5/G,
    A_H = 16 pi G^2 E_*^2 x^2/c^8.
The massless Hawking flux scales as A_H T_H^4, including a dimensionless
coefficient for species and frequency-dependent transmission through
the exterior gravitational field after frequency integration. Thus
P_H=P_0 x^(-2) for a positive normalization P_0 (units of power).
By energy balance E_* dx/dt=-P_H. With gamma=P_0/E_* and x(0)=x_0,

    x(t)^3=x_0^3-3 gamma t,
    x(t)/x_0=1-gamma t/x_0^3+O((gamma t/x_0^3)^2).

limit at fixed t. The absolute speed scales as x^(-2), the fractional

with the electron calibration of section 14. Power carries different

75. ORDINARY STANDARD MODEL AND ITS MAP TO CHARGED-FERMION MASSES

Convention. Use the minimal renormalizable Standard Model in flat
spacetime, with one Higgs doublet and three matter generations.
Use natural units and Q_em=T3+Y. The local gauge algebra is
su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)_Y. It gives eight gluon fields, three weak
gauge fields and one hypercharge field. The following are the color
and weak representations and hypercharges in a right-handed singlet
convention. Each matter row occurs once per generation.

    field       (SU(3), SU(2), Y)
    Q_L          (3, 2,  1/6)
    u_R          (3, 1,  2/3)
    d_R          (3, 1, -1/3)
    L_L          (1, 2, -1/2)
    e_R          (1, 1, -1)
    H            (1, 2,  1/2)  one scalar doublet in total

The renormalizable Lagrangian has gauge and matter kinetic terms,
the covariant Higgs kinetic term, the quadratic and quartic Higgs
potential, three charged-sector Yukawa interactions, and the allowed
strong CP term. The conventional generic parameter count is 19:
3 gauge couplings, 2 Higgs potential parameters, 9 Yukawa singular
values, 4 CKM parameters and 1 physical strong CP angle. With this
field content and renormalizability the three neutrinos are massless.
Accounting for observed neutrino oscillations requires a mass extension.

PL-51. Charged mass projection. Use the flavor order of sections
57 and 60, and choose compatible mass definitions. At tree level
write H=(0,(v+h)/sqrt(2))^T in unitary gauge, with v>0. Substitution
in the Yukawa interactions gives mass matrices M_a=(v/sqrt(2))Y_a
for a=e,u,d. Write s_i(Y_a) for singular values labeled by the
corresponding mass eigenstates. Biunitary diagonalization gives

    m_ai = v s_i(Y_a)/sqrt(2)
      iff   s_i(Y_a) = sqrt(2) m_ai/v.                       (75.1)

Given any nine positive labeled masses and fixed v, diagonal Yukawa
matrices with entries sqrt(2)m_ai/v realize them. Changing a relative
left-handed quark rotation preserves these masses while changing CKM.
Thus (75.1) is invertible on the nine singular values for fixed v.
Lifting masses back to a complete SM parameter point has many solutions.
The same expansion yields the coefficient of -h fbar f in the mass
basis, g_hff=m_f/v. With m_f and v fixed, a separately measured Higgs
rate can test this coupling after the rate and its corrections are
modeled. Comparing precise pole or running mass extractions requires
scheme, scale, radiative corrections and matching as appropriate.

PL-52. On these nine mass observables the minimal SM spans the positive
orthant. The fixed bare lattice gives the ray m=K ell, K>0, for the
nine declared coefficients ell. The orthant contains this ray strictly.
For positive masses membership is equivalent to the eight equations
m_j-(ell_j/ell_e)m_e=0 for j other than e. Each row has its own
target coordinate, proving independence. Thus fitting the nine masses
with nine unconstrained SM mass parameters leaves zero residual degrees
of freedom for that fit. With comparable observations and positive
definite joint covariance the lattice contrasts have rank eight, as
in section 72. No numerical fit or new empirical input enters this
identification. In particular m_mu/m_e=256 is a restriction of the
frozen lattice assignment. An electron anchor alone leaves the SM
muon Yukawa free.

The sixteen model addresses enter through the quotient of section 61.
The present comparison identifies nine marked flavor labels and mass
observables. Reconstructing SM gauge representations, currents and
mixing from these masses alone is obstructed by the nonuniqueness
above. PL-51 and PL-52 are B-tier mass-sector identifications.

76. A DEGENERATE HORIZON SECTOR, REVERSIBLE EMISSION AND DETAILED BALANCE
======================================================================

Question. A single mass measurement can be shared by many orthogonal
states. Can that multiplicity both preserve quantum information during
evaporation and determine the spectrum of the outgoing radiation?

Conditional inputs and indices. The bare high-energy address is n with
E_n=E_* n. Its bare eigenspace has dimension one for n>1 (PL-09).
Introduce a separate area label j>=0 and a physical internal register
B_j=(C^4)^(tensor j), B_0=C, with d_j=4^j states of the same energy E_j.
This exact microstate hypothesis strengthens section 74's asymptotic
count; the four digit choices used to encode n alone do not imply it.
Take A_j=alpha l_P^2 j, alpha>0, and the Schwarzschild relation
E_j=E_P sqrt(alpha j/(16 pi)), E_P^2=hbar c^5/G. A mass address may
label a coarse energy band; exact alignments are governed by PL-50.
The terminal one-dimensional sector is a boundary condition of the toy
model. Applying semiclassical gravity through the last step is a separate
physical extrapolation.

PL-53. Entropy fixes a consistency condition on the area spacing.
The entropy of the uniform distribution on the orthogonal states at j
is S_j/k_B=ln d_j=j ln 4, or exactly 2j bits. The Schwarzschild area
law would assign S_j/k_B=A_j/(4l_P^2)=alpha j/4. The two coefficients
agree if and only if alpha=4 ln 4. This uses the independent area law
to match the normalizations. Density of distinct energy levels alone
supplies no multiplicity at a fixed energy.

PL-54. A reversible, energy-preserving emission operator exists.
Fix 1<=s<=j. Suppose the outgoing channel has 4^s orthogonal states
labeled by the s-digit words u, all of energy epsilon=E_j-E_(j-s).
They can denote a radiation packet. A single particle with precisely
these modes would require its own physical construction. Let a denote
a (j-s)-digit prefix and v an s-digit suffix. Define

    L_u |a,v> = delta_(u,v)|a>,
    V_(j,s) psi = sum_u (L_u psi) tensor |u>_out.

Each input basis state maps to |a> tensor |v>_out. Every output basis
state is attained exactly once. Consequently V_(j,s) is unitary between
these equal-dimensional sectors. It preserves superpositions and
entanglement with an arbitrary external reference. The final sector has
total energy E_(j-s)+epsilon=E_j, taking the radiation input as vacuum.
This realizes the least outgoing dimension allowed by an isometry:
d_j <= d_(j-s) d_out requires d_out>=4^s, or 2s bits of capacity.
For s=1 at each step one reaches B_0; concatenation in the reverse
emission order reconstructs the initial state coherently. This proves
complete recovery within the specified model. For the uniform initial
state, the outgoing label is uniform on its fixed-energy shell. This
alone supplies no information about probabilities of different energies.

PL-55. A resolved forward/reverse strength ratio follows from state count.
Use uniform initial black-hole ensembles on each side, one specified
radiation mode u, matched external normalization, and reciprocal
matrix elements. For any nonzero transition map L:B_j -> B_(j-s)
and its absorption adjoint, define the reduced strengths

    w_em  = Tr_(B_j)(L^dagger L)/d_j,
    w_abs = Tr_(B_(j-s))(L L^dagger)/d_(j-s).

The traces equal the same sum of squared matrix elements. Hence

    w_em/w_abs = d_(j-s)/d_j = 4^(-s).                      (76.1)

For the suffix operator, L_u L_u^dagger=I and L_u^dagger L_u is a
projection of rank 4^(j-s). Its reduced strengths are 4^(-s) and 1.
Equation (76.1) is a quotient for a specified mode and its reverse.
A sum over modes, an ambient population, and a physical rate per unit
time each require the corresponding factors and normalization.

PL-56. The entropy factor has an exact finite-energy thermal form.
Impose the consistency condition alpha=4 ln 4. Put E=E_j and F=E_(j-s)
and beta_H(y)=1/[k_B T_H(y)]=8 pi y/E_P^2. Then

    s ln 4 = 4 pi (E^2-F^2)/E_P^2
           = beta_H((E+F)/2) (E-F)
           = beta_H(E) epsilon (1-epsilon/(2E)).

Thus (76.1) is exactly exp[-beta_H((E+F)/2) epsilon]. At fixed s
and j tending to infinity, epsilon/E=1-sqrt(1-s/j) tends to zero
and beta_H(E) epsilon tends to s ln 4. This yields the Hawking
Boltzmann factor for a resolved forward/reverse comparison in the
large-area limit. The entropy secant and the midpoint formula are exact
at finite j in this Schwarzschild model.

PL-57. Reversibility and detailed balance leave the spectral weights free.
At any j>=2 choose any probability vector p_s, 1<=s<=j, and keep
different outgoing jump sizes in distinguishable energy sectors. Set

    V_j(p) psi = direct_sum_s sqrt(p_s) V_(j,s) psi.

Orthogonality of the sectors gives V_j(p)^dagger V_j(p)=sum_s p_s I=I.
Every branch conserves total energy. The probability of a jump of
size s is exactly p_s for every normalized initial state. Each active
resolved matrix element is multiplied by sqrt(p_s) in both directions,
so (76.1) continues to hold. For example, for j>=2, setting p_1=1
or p_2=1 yields different emitted energies from the same initial mass.
Choosing different strictly positive vectors gives different spectra
even with the same allowed channels. For repeated coherent emission,
retain the packet order and distinguishable branch records. Composition
on the remaining hole preserves inner products, and each history reaches
B_0 after at most j jumps. Its final radiation can encode the entire
input. The construction sets no waiting times or observational couplings.

First unresolved physical edge. Neither the bare mass spectrum nor the
conditional state count selects the p_s, the physical radiation mode
spaces, line widths or clock. Replacing a known transition map by a
nonzero rescaling also preserves (76.1) while changing its individual
strengths. Matching the Hawking flux requires a physical interaction,
the accessible species and modes, and their frequency-dependent
transmission (greybody factors). Recovering information for a real
evaporating hole also requires that its complete dynamics implement an
isometry into the asymptotic radiation. PL-54 proves existence for the
chosen internal register and channels. PL-57 proves that this existence
and the balance law cannot select the physical spectrum. The physical
black-hole information paradox remains open for the mass lattice.

Static checks. For j=1,...,5 and every 1<=s<=j, finite word enumeration
verified the basis bijection and trace ratio. Numerical substitution at
the same 15 pairs checked the midpoint identity. The arguments above
establish the conditional results for all admitted j and s.

78. RAPID FORMATION, SLOW EMISSION AND FINITE-TIME DISTINGUISHABILITY
======================================================================

What can an exterior observer distinguish? Two formation histories can
end with the same measured mass and other bulk parameters. Their prompt
gravitational or electromagnetic signals may still differ. State which
formation data and outgoing modes the observer receives. Reversibility
means that the full evolution preserves quantum states and their inner
products. It specifies neither a radiation clock nor a way to reconstruct
the state from a partial observation.

PL-58. An exactly recoverable phase with invisible early radiation.
In PL-54 take j>=2 and successive single-digit emissions. In the
two-dimensional subspace spanned by |0> and |1> of each digit, prepare

    |psi_+> = (|0>^tensor j + |1>^tensor j)/sqrt(2),
    |psi_-> = (|0>^tensor j - |1>^tensor j)/sqrt(2).

These states are orthogonal and have the same energy E_j. Write rho_k^+
and rho_k^- for the respective density matrices of the first k emitted
digits. For 0<k<j at least one digit remains in the hole. The inner
product of the two remaining strings is zero. Taking the partial trace
therefore removes the cross terms and both radiation states become

    (|0...0><0...0| + |1...1><1...1|)/2.

For k=0 both radiation states are vacuum. For k=j the two radiation
states are pure and orthogonal. Thus their trace distance, defined by
D(rho,sigma)=||rho-sigma||_1/2, is zero for k<j and one for k=j.
A measurement of the product of the Pauli X operators on the collected
two-level subspaces recovers the sign at completion. The transfer
preserves an arbitrary superposition on the full input space; the timing
result concerns this particular encoded sign.

PL-59. Timed observation with approximately equal formation records.
Let K(t) be the number of emissions by time t after formation. Assume its
distribution p_k(t) is independent of the alternative input, and k is a
classical record in orthogonal observed sectors. For the preceding pair,
with no additional distinguishing record, the outgoing trace distance is

    D_out(t)=sum_k p_k(t) D(rho_k^+,rho_k^-)=Pr[K(t)=j]=F(t).

This follows from additivity of the trace norm on block diagonal
operators. With equal prior probabilities the optimal discrimination
success probability is (1+D_out)/2. In particular a recorded, reversible
process can remain arbitrarily close to chance for a finite observation
if its completion probability in that window is small.

More generally suppose that, conditioned on each incomplete record k,
the JOINT state of the observed formation data and all collected
radiation differs by trace distance at most delta, 0<=delta<=1.
For complete records its distance is at most one. Then

    P_guess(t) <= [1 + delta + (1-delta) F(t)]/2.             (78.1)

This is a condition on all data accessible jointly. Similar marginal
formation records alone do not bound correlations with early radiation.
Ignoring the count or applying detector noise can lower the attainable
distinguishability. State-dependent timing can itself distinguish the
alternatives; (78.1) uses the common count distribution above.

For this model assume cumulative emitted energy from evaporation is
nonnegative, with E_j-E_0 released on every complete history. Its mean
by time t, B(t), is then at least F(t)(E_j-E_0), so F(t) is bounded by
min(1,B(t)/(E_j-E_0)). This gives an upper bound on the observable phase
distinction when the expected energy budget is small. If data are lost
to the detector, detected energy alone does not provide that upper bound.

Scope test. For the same energy sequence and the same clock, choose the
two basis words with identical prefixes and distinct last digits.
The first emitted digit distinguishes this pair perfectly. Its trace
distance at time t is Pr[K(t)>=1] under the same count assumption.
Release time depends on the encoded distinction and the interaction.
Slow mass loss alone leaves the first time information appears undetermined.

Physical obstruction. In classical stellar collapse matter pressure
enters the dynamics. Causal trapping at a horizon limits which signals
reach infinity. Pressure and the mass alone supply no clock for decoding
quantum correlations. Nor do equal late masses imply identical formation
signals. Identifying (78.1) with real black holes requires an actual
formation map, its accessible prompt records, and an emission dynamics
that fixes both p_k(t) and the conditional early states.
